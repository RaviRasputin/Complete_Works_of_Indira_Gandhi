Speech by Prime Minister Shrimati Indira Gandhi from the ramparts of the Red Fort on the occasion of Independence Day

Date: 15-8-1976

Citizens of India, my brothers and sisters, dear children,

When I hoist the flag here, I am reminded of the day when Pandit Nehru first hoisted the flag of free India here. I am reminded of the hardships, suffering and sacrifices through which this country passed, and of those brave people on whose strength he hoisted his flag here. When we look back over these years, we see that we have come a good distance along the road we had set before ourselves. Along the way there have been some failings, some mistakes, some shortfalls in work; but the further we have advanced, the more we have seen how long the road ahead is. We have seen that with every step the difficulties do not lessen but increase; we have seen that some paths, once completed, give rise to many more paths. But such is the road of life. Our effort has been that we take our steps forward on the very path that our great leaders placed before us and before the country.

There are many kinds of freedom. We demanded full independence (purna swaraj) from Jawaharlal Nehru's time [sic: "from Jawaharlal"], and attained it, and that was right, because freedom of any other kind cannot truly be called freedom. Purna swaraj means not merely that you elect your representative; it does not mean merely that whoever wishes may speak as loudly as he likes, even if he voices very few people, or has great ability in speaking. Purna swaraj means that the fruits of swaraj, the results of swaraj, reach every home in India. They reach those people whose

[page 2]
voice cannot be raised, those people whom, perhaps, we do not even properly know what they want, those people who until today have remained deprived of all facilities. Towards this swaraj we are trying to take our country forward. But in making this road, many obstacles come up. In some class, some group, or some individual's interest, there is always some jolt or blow. We want life to improve for everyone, whether by a large measure or a small one. But if you reflect, you will yourselves feel that one who already has something gets still more, while one who has nothing, what will he get? This is why our programme is for all Indians, but greater attention is given to those sections that are called weak, whatever the reason for their weakness: caste, economic condition, or the backwardness of some part of the country.

So at every step we collide with someone or other. We have taken those collisions, but we did not let our feet falter, and today I am happy to say how much progress our country has made in these past years.

We placed one programme before the people. Part of it belonged to the government and part to the people. For a new discipline had come, a new feeling and hope arose, expectation was born in the hearts of the people, and all gave cooperation in this work. I do not say that the work has been completed, or that as much has been done as we thought, as much as we wanted; that has not happened. But even then, firm, strong steps have been taken forward and the work is going on. It is not uniform everywhere; where the people are awake and the workers are strong, there this work is advancing faster. You all know how large production has been. Nearly 7 per cent was the progress our country made.

[page 3]
Similarly, there was the programme of giving house sites to the landless poor, under which 70 lakh families have been given sites. This is a great deal, but there is some slowness. The programme of [illegible] is advancing fast and we hope it will advance faster. The land reform programme too has not gone as far as we wanted. This is not a new programme; it is very old. But when it is placed in the hands of officials, then wherever land is being redistributed, new questions arise. Because those far-off places are beyond our eyes' reach, and people do not give proper attention to the facts, there are many mistakes too. I know all this, and that is why every year here and elsewhere I say the same thing: until the people, whether farmers, labourers, poor, or any other citizens, themselves take it in hand that wherever there is injustice they will draw attention to it, not merely by way of complaint, but by drawing attention to say "a mistake has been made here, and in this way we and you together can set this mistake right", until then [it will not be remedied]. This feeling has grown somewhat. We can say that in the past year the people's welfare that has been done, the awakening that has come among the people, was not there earlier. But I do not mean to say that nothing was done before. If earlier no strong foundation had been laid for the country's industry and agriculture, then we could not have made this progress in the past year. It is also true that even with a foundation, if there is no discipline, and if the feeling of responsibility does not come into the people's hearts, then on whatever strong foundation, we cannot build a beautiful India. You will remember how much indiscipline there had been before that, whether in schools and colleges, in the fields, or on the roads of our cities. And in those times of economic hardship, the work of essential commodities [illegible]

[page 4]
When it was like that earlier, production came to a standstill, because on all sides slogans of strike were raised, slogans of revolution were raised. Now you know that we too believe in revolution. Gandhiji said: I believe that these were all great revolutionaries of this country. But the path of his revolution was the path of non-violence, the path of friendship, the path of peace; and if we had strayed from that path at that time, however brave and capable some people might have been, if we had wandered from his path, perhaps India would not be free today.

But because we planted our feet on Gandhiji's path and moved forward, we made this country free, we laid the foundation of democracy. We have run industrialisation fast and in every way have increased the country's strength and might. However much people may make accusations, however much they may condemn India, no one in the world can deny that India is today as strong, in every way, as it has never been before. And no one can say that India could have advanced without [illegible], no one can say that without working hard, without increasing our industry, or giving the people some comfort and rest, we could have done it.

We thought that once the fight against Bangladesh [sic] / the fight of freedom was fought, we would be free for ever. But what do we see? Sometimes there are indeed attacks on us in the open, but more of them are those that are made secretly, which are not seen. Even before, we were seeing and hearing this, but in these past years much of it has come out into the open. Those people who did not give India their hand, when there was no restriction here, no one had been arrested, no newspaper was under any ban, even then those governments and those people did not help India; but to those countries where there was a military government or some other kind of dictatorial government, the people's voice

[page 5]
could not rise at all. In that period, such countries received help of that kind. No one raised a voice that murder is being committed in a certain place, not one man or two men but hundreds, in places thousands, in places lakhs. Those same people are now the ones who point their fingers at India.

But you will remember that this is not the first time. When Gandhiji was alive, many kinds of things were said about him as well. After him, things were said about Jawaharlal Nehru, and after that, when I had not yet even become a leader [sic], things were said about India. Why should India explode an atomic device? A great danger is hanging over the whole world, and other countries may amass on us whatever they like, spend crores of rupees; there is no danger from them, no condemnation of them; on the contrary, efforts are being made to see how their weapons can be sold. But if we ourselves do any work to improve our economic condition, that is called wrong work. When we began the work of fertiliser, it was said that in a poor country, where villages have so many difficulties, what need is there for fertiliser and tractors? Now you know that the foundation of any modern country is fertiliser; the farmer's needs are met, the factories' needs are met, and in good ways the demands of ordinary people are met by it. If you had not laid all this work, this foundation, this base, and Jawaharlal Nehru had not, then we would have been begging for alms in all directions. But even now there are many things we need, and even so we do not beg alms from anyone.

A few years ago people used to say that India could not rid itself of the shortage of grain, but you know that 12 crore tons have been produced, and ten years ago our difficulty was not that there was no grain; the difficulty was how to keep it, where to keep it. So it is true that there has been good rain, but it is also true that if government

[page 6]
help did not reach, and if our hardworking farmers did not take that help and prepare to adopt new methods, then this production would not have been possible. For a few years there has been a shortage of rain, drought has come, especially in the south, in Tamil Nadu, and elsewhere, and in some places there is too much rain. So our farmers are suffering great hardship and my full sympathy is with them. But we know that they will face it; we know that they are strong, and that the new knowledge, science and technique that we are giving them will enable us to face all these things too. What has to be seen is that this programme has not yet reached every village, and it has to be seen how it can be taken there. It has to be seen that there is a programme against [drought]. What need is there for mountain students? What need is there in the desert, where heavy rain falls, where floods come, what need is there there? In this way our methods differ. In this way, in each of our own areas, it is not that everyone says there should be a fertiliser factory here or there and a big factory there; but whatever resources we have, they should be used fully. If this feeling takes root, then our country and our people will progress faster and faster.

This year, in the public sector, there are those factories which used to be much criticised in our newspapers, and our big industrialists would always say that a government cannot run factories at all, that they themselves incur only losses. Many of them have made very good profits, and their production has been very large. This increase has come in our production, and this is our entire hope, that those who come from the public sector factories and those that are lagging, whose performance is still not satisfactory, even those are quickly improving their work; a full effort is being made. So every time, in every direction, we have to look, and this will happen when every citizen understands his duty. I want to give my

[page 7]
thanks to labourer brothers and sisters, because through their work this improvement and this growth has come.

A few hours from now I am going this evening to meet the Ministers [sic]. You know that a big meeting is taking place there, a conference of the non-aligned countries, and the main question before all of us is how to keep our independence safe. Independence not only of borders, but independence of our ideology, independence of our own decision, independence of making our own selves strong, of developing and progressing in our own country. The main thing is this: because we are all facing the same question, [non-alignment]

when we help one another, when we cooperate with one another, a great power comes. Gurudev Tagore said that even the strength of the weak, if they stand with firmness, with unity, becomes a mighty force, and our experience has been the same. In the time of the independence movement, what was the strength of the people of India? Only poverty, only slavery, fear. But because we became one, because we were not moved by condemnation or by praise, we stood firm on our path, then this was a power greater than the greatest power of the world at that time, greater than our poor farmers, labourers, students; their strength, even greater than those of the factories, grew. This strength we need today in the poor countries. The difficulty is that some countries are such that they are swayed by others' words. They think that if you give some help, then they will help. They do not see whether this help is of such kind that it does not touch their independence even slightly; if some were to be made strong by help alone, they will go on asking for help in future too. We too have taken help from all countries, from all kinds of governments, ideologies, from the countries and from the world. But in one thing we stood firm. Our policy is this, our ideology is this, that in this way we want to take our country forward and the

[page 8]
help that we want to be given, seeing that, we accept the help that is given. But we do not want to take help on any condition. This is what we said and we have remained firm on it, and even in the future, if there is condemnation of us from you, it is not because some arms have been given or some few big [people] have come to hold forth on newspapers. It is because this country stands firm, that a poor country is advancing with firmness, without caring for anyone else. And in the same way we have to go ahead. And those who condemn us, I want to say to them only this: that however much they condemn, however much effort they make to weaken us, that will become our strength; from that we will become still stronger and with even more speed continue on our path.

In these past years you have seen that whenever we were attacked, we did not bow down; however much pressure they brought on us, even if mountains of difficulty fell upon us, we faced it and went ahead. This country, the history of India, has been a history of struggle, a history of difficulties. And at the same time India has been a country of creativity, and a country of peace. But along with that, remember that it has been a country of heroism, a country of firmness, a country of ideals, and every citizen has to remember this history of ours. It is to be thought that we are the representatives of new India, we are the symbols of India, we are the signs of new India! Whether our honoured children, whether young, whether old, this responsibility rests on the shoulders of every one, and each person who tries to understand these things (who does what work, who does what business) in life, these are very small things. One question only is before us: on what road can we take the country forward? Whether someone says good or says bad, from this standpoint we have to move forward. If our beautiful children do not study properly, if they take no interest in education, then neither their body can become strong nor their mind can become strong. And this young generation, if they are not strong, if this constructive work

[page 9]
is not learnt in education, and love of country, national feeling does not grow in them, then that will be the greatest weakness, and from that the greatest harm to the country.

Countries keep their borders safe, and the reason for this is that we take pride in our armies. But an army can fight only with bravery, and behind it there must be a brave, strong people. So on this day let us look at our history, look at our present, look at our future, and let us resolve that whichever direction the country goes, however strong it becomes, however much progress it makes, this is not the responsibility of anyone else, nor will it happen with anyone else's help; it will be from our own hands and feet, from the strength of our own hearts, the sharpness of our own minds. All of us, you and we, have to do this, and if this feeling is there, then no one can ever push India back. I have full faith that this small discipline that has come, we will increase it with our own strength.

For some years now the situation of emergency has been here; will it go back? It is not that people do not see that for many months there has been improvement in it. And what has been the result of the improvement? Did those people who were doing the destruction, with this kind of agitation, with stopping of work, do something that brought benefit to the country? Or are they still determined to walk the same path? This question we have to ask. It is not in our hands what will happen; it is in the hands of those people who were engaged in breaking the country, who were engaged in spreading indiscipline, who were encouraging thieves and lawbreakers, who have never believed in democracy, nor in the kind of freedom we believe in, nor in secularism, who believe in fighting communalism [sic], who have never been in agreement with these ideologies; those people have encouraged these movements.

So we have to see that when we are going forward, a very great

[page 10]
many years after, for the first time programmes for the poor have begun and are advancing successfully, though not as fast as we wish. In a big country, where there are crores and crores of people, 70 lakh families having been given house sites is very little. But where there was not even one before, 70 lakh is large. And if we say that even those 70 lakh are not to be given, then how will the others get it?

So this is the hope we have, that we must think about these things deeply. We do not want that on the part of the government or any organisation it should be only thus and we have made efforts. We want that every one, thinking, with discipline, serving the country, engage in this. In fact it is known that villagers always said that if one has no right, then nothing comes of duty. Those who have rights, understanding their duties, then there will be the dream of true democracy: a democracy in which the voice of the poorest of the poor is heard, in which no one, merely because of economic or social weakness, will fall behind others, in which everyone feels that if we have rights, then our poor brothers and sisters who are backward, they too have rights, and that as much as I have to fight for my own rights, so much I have to fight for their rights. When this feeling comes, then rights can be obtained by all. So long as we think that some people should have rights, the voices of some people should be raised, and others, whatever happens to them, in the same way we lost our freedom, and that day will come again to us.

So I would only say that remember that this India is a land of sacrifice. Today we do not need that kind of sacrifice; today we need courage, that with courage, with firmness, we make a new life for our country, and in this great work, if we all join together, we can lift our country very high. And keep in mind that country does not mean only earth or

[page 11]
mountains, because we love them too and are engaged in making them beautiful and improving them; but when we say country, then it means every one of the crores and crores of people, to lift them up.

To bring happiness and comfort to their homes, to give their children every kind of facility, this is what we have to do. On this great journey, may you walk with us step by step, shoulder to shoulder; this is my heartfelt wish today.

I extend to you my greetings on this day and good wishes for the future. Raise your voices with me, all of you, children, old, young, men and women. Say Jai Hind.

Jai Hind! Jai Hind!! Jai Hind!!!

Say: Long live Mother India!
